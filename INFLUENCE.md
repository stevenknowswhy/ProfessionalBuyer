# Influence Profile: who carries the panel

11 judges deliberate together: 4:30–4:45 they pick the top 6 from all submissions,
4:45–5:15 those teams demo (2 min + 1 min Q&A), 5:15–5:25 judges pick 1st/2nd/3rd + side quests.
Side-quest winners are nominated *during* judging and voted after.

## Panel dynamics

- **Coalition logic.** 8 of 11 judges are tool vendors. A project using N co-host tools gets N
  champions; a project using none gets none. Breadth of *meaningful* integration beats depth
  on one tool.
- **Status cascade.** When Shamgunov / Johnston / da Costa signal approval, others defer —
  especially the younger founders.
- **15-minute triage.** Top-6 selection is pattern recognition: recognizable stacks, a working
  demo, a memorable one-liner. "The agent that finds what your household overpays for"
  beats the 40th "JARVIS."
- **Public nominations.** Side-quest nominations happen during judging — they're brand signals
  too (Thorelli wants CodeRabbit attached to great OSS).
- **The a16z veto/crown.** da Costa can kill with "but is it a company?" — and crown with
  the same question. Always have the company answer ready.
- **Differentiation multiplier (not in the matrix).** MATRIX.md scores fit in a vacuum; judging
  is relative. A crowded category (Chief of Staff / JARVIS) means competing against 30 similar
  demos. A distinctive idea only has to be good. This is the core strategic bet behind the Buyer.
